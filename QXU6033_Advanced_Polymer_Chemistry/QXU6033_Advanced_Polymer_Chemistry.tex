\subsection{Towards Living Polymerisation}

As long as “living” polymer is kept tightly closed (no \ce{H2O}, \ce{ROH}, \ce{CO2}, \ce{O2}) more monomer can be added and the molecular weight will increase.

\begin{enumerate}
    \item MW increases steadily with time/conversion
    \item Narrow PDI($<1.2$ or $1.3$)
    \item Can add more monomer or another monomer/blocks.
\end{enumerate}

\subsubsection*{Kinetic Issue}

To make FRP "living", need to inhibit termination and chain-transfer without affecting initiation/propagation too much.

\subsubsection*{Strategy 1: Reversible Termination}

Also, Reversible Deactivation.

$$
\sim P_n \cdot + X \cdot \ce{<=>[k_d][k_a]} \sim P_n -X \qquad (\text{"Dormant(sleep) FR"})
$$

\textbf{Note:} Do not use Strategy 1, either refer to specific chemistry (NMP, or TEMPO) or "reversible deactivation"

\subsubsection*{Strategy 2: Catalytic/Assisted Reversible Termination}

$$
\sim P_n \cdot + X-Y \cdot \ce{<=>[k_d][k_a]} \sim P_n -X +Y\qquad (\text{"Dormant(sleep) FR"})
$$

X can be single atom transferred to $P\cdot$, Y normally a transition metal.

Also, \textbf{Catalytic Reversible Deactivation.}

\subsubsection*{Reversible Chain-Transfer(Degenerative Transfer)}

$$
\sim P_n \cdot + \sim P_m-X \ce{<=>[k_d][k_a]} \sim P_n-X + \sim P_m \cdot
$$

In processes $\sim P_n \cdot$ or $\sim P_m \cdot$ themselves, \textbf{Termination still occurs}.

\begin{mynote_env}[frametitle=To be effective]
    Reversible Termination should be $k_d > k_a$\\
    Reversible Chain-Transfer is $k_d \sim  k_a$
\end{mynote_env}
